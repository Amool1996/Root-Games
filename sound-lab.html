What changed in the chain

  • A limiter on the output. The old mix summed straight into the speakers and clipped on busy beats — that crackle is most of what reads as "annoying".
  • Every voice is filtered. The old engine used bare oscillators; a raw square (the wheel) keeps every harsh harmonic. Now each note has its own lowpass, and the music bus has a brightness control per screen.
  • A short room. A 1.7 s reverb at 14–42% depending on screen. This is the single biggest "toy → instrument" step.
  • Four-bar patterns, not two, with a variation in bars 3–4, plus ±6 ms and ±9% wobble on every note, so it stops sounding like a machine.
  • The hats are gone. Eight per bar of 7 kHz noise was the most fatiguing thing in the mix. Grooves are now a soft kick, an oom-pah brush or a shaker on one 16th in eight.
  • Long-dwell screens calm themselves. Title after 45 s, leaderboard after 20 s, thin out to ~45% instead of nagging a queue.
  • Real instruments per place — marimba, music box, filtered reed, warm pad — so where you are is audible, not just how fast.
